                  If the farmer uses money instead of wheat as a medium of
                  exchange, he can keep the money for a longer time and use
                  it for making purchases later. So, money acts as a store of
                  value that can be used later. Isn’t it fascinating!
                  Money also serves as a common denomination that
                  measures the value of goods and services, and enables the
                  comparison of goods and services in terms of their prices.
                  For example, your parents pay money to the shopkeepers
                  in exchange for various products. This money is used by the
                  shopkeepers to pay salaries to their workers. The workers
                  use the same medium to buy everyday essentials and other
                  goods, pay their children’s school fees, etc.
