## 1. Idioms & Expressions

- **Definition**: An idiom is a multi-word phrase whose meaning cannot be deduced by simply combining the literal definitions of its individual words. For example, *"in plain sight"* literally means something is positioned where eyes can see it, but idiomatically it means *"clearly visible without any attempt at concealment yet easily overlooked"* (as explored in Class 5/6 English).
- **Student Objective**:
  1. **Comprehension**: Learn to look past literal interpretations and understand contextual figures of speech.
  2. **Application**: Incorporate idioms into essays, stories, and spoken English to elevate descriptive writing and conversational fluency.

## 2. Synonyms & Antonyms (Case Study: "Frantic")

- **Synonyms (Words with Similar Meanings)**:
  - **Frantic**: Panicked, frenzied, distressed, overwrought, frantic. (In the context of *Papa's Spectacles*, Papa's frantic search reflects anxiety and panic).
- **Antonyms (Words with Opposite Meanings)**:
  - **Frantic**: Calm, serene, composed, relaxed, tranquil.

## 3. Word Forms (Morphology)

- **Definition**: Word forms refer to the systematic variations of a root base word across different grammatical categories (parts of speech), tenses, or numbers.
- **Examples**:
  - *Noun form*: Search (the action/noun)
  - *Verb forms*: Search -> Searched (past) -> Searching (continuous)
  - *Adjectival form*: Searchable
- **Student Objective**: Master morphological transformations to ensure correct subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, and vocabulary range in sentence construction.
